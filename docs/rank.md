# `stats test`: the rank, variance and exact tests (milestone S4)

**Status: designed 2026-10-24 before any code of S4; S4.1 (`anova`), S4.2 (`kruskal`), S4.3 (`mannwhitney`) and S4.4 (`wilcoxon`) S4.5 (`ks`) and S4.6 (`fisher`) are built and measured (sections 9 to 14); the other kinds are design only and nothing about them has been measured; what it reads from the code of S0 to S3 is marked [read], every bound is a [choice] fixed before the first run and corrected in place, in this document, if a run shows it false.** Tags: **[read]** read from a document or source, **[measured]** printed by a script (sections 9 to 14 for `anova`, `kruskal`, `mannwhitney`, `wilcoxon`, `ks` and `fisher`), **[choice]** a decision the maintainer can overturn, **[open]** not decided. S4 is the rest of the table of `docs/design.md` section 4: `anova`, `kruskal`, `mannwhitney`, `wilcoxon`, `ks` and `fisher`. The conventions of `docs/test.md` (the answer, the flags `--cols`, `--group`, `--pairs`, `--level`, `--correct`, `--max-tests`, the rule tags, the gates and the discipline of the mutants and the fingerprint) are not repeated; this document says what is new. Issue #5.

## 1. What it adds

`stats test KIND FILE` with six more kinds. The answer is the one of `docs/test.md` section 1 (`statistic`, `df`, `p_value`, `p_method`, `effect_size`, `interval`, `n`, `method`, `assumptions`, `reason`); `p_method` says which of the exact and the asymptotic p-value was used, and `--correct` works over any of them.

| kind | question | inputs | statistic | p-value |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `anova` | do `k >= 2` groups have the same mean? | `--cols A,B,..` `--group G` (`k` levels, at most `--max-groups`), `--variance welch\|pooled` | `F` (Welch's `F*` by default, the classic `F` with `pooled`) | `f_sf` |
| `kruskal` | do `k >= 2` groups have the same distribution (location)? | as `anova` | `H`, tie-corrected | `chi2_sf` on `k - 1` |
| `mannwhitney` | does a value of group `a` tend to exceed one of group `b`? | `--cols A,B,..` `--group G` (two levels) | `U` of group `a` | exact, or normal with tie and continuity correction |
| `wilcoxon` | is the median of the differences zero? | `--pairs A:B,..` or `--cols A,.. --mu0 m` | `W+`, the sum of the ranks of the positive differences | exact, or normal with tie and continuity correction |
| `ks` | do two groups have the same distribution? | `--cols A,B,..` `--group G` (two levels) | `D`, the largest distance of the two empirical distribution functions | exact, or the Kolmogorov distribution |
| `fisher` | are two binary columns independent? (2 x 2) | `--cols A --vs G`, each with exactly two levels | the sample odds ratio | exact: the hypergeometric probabilities of the tables as or less likely than the observed one |

## 2. What each computes **[choice]**

**Ranks and ties.** Ranks are the average ranks of `lib/corr/ranks.cho` **[read]**; a rank sum is kept as the integer `2 R` (doubled ranks), so every statistic below is an exact rational of integers and is rounded once. The size of each tie group `t_j` is counted from the sorted order keys, which are exact (`rank.key_of`, **[read]**). Missing cells are dropped as in S3, and the answer says how many (`n`).

* **`anova`** (built in S4.1; what was built is in section 9, and this paragraph says it as it is). With `n_i`, `S1_i`, `S2_i` per group (the exact power sums of `describe`, **[read]**), `L_i = n_i S2_i - S1_i^2` (the sum of squares of group `i` is `L_i / n_i`), `N`, `LT = N S2 - S1^2` and the common denominator `D = prod n_i`, the exact integers `SSW = N sum L_i D / n_i`, `SST = LT D`, `SSB = SST - SSW` (all over `N D`) give `F = (SSB (N - k)) / (SSW (k - 1))` on `(k - 1, N - k)` degrees of freedom, **one rounding of an exact rational**; the sum is folded group by group as a fraction (`num = num n_i + L_i den`, `den = den n_i`), and `D` has at most `k log2(N / k)` bits (below 13,300 at `k = 1,000`, `N = 10^7`), inside the 28,600 bits of `acc.central`. The effect sizes `eta^2 = SSB / SST` and `omega^2 = (SSB (N - k) - (k - 1) SSW) / (SST (N - k) + SSW)` are likewise one rounding each (the second can be negative: it is printed as it is, not clipped) and are **always the classic ones**, whichever `--variance` is asked. **Welch's `F*`** (the default, as Welch is the default of `t2`) has weights `w_i = n_i / s_i^2 = n_i^2 (n_i - 1) / L_i`, `W = sum w_i`, `T = sum (1 - w_i / W)^2 / (n_i - 1)` and `F* = (B / (k - 1)) / (1 + 2 (k - 2) T / (k^2 - 1))`, `df2 = (k^2 - 1) / (3 T)`, where `B = sum w_i (m_i - m_w)^2`. **[choice, corrected in place]** The first draft of this paragraph said the terms are exact rationals "summed in double-double"; the first run showed that the *form* matters: `B` through the weighted mean `m_w` subtracts nearly equal numbers (a group of very small variance has all the weight, `m_w` is its mean rounded to 2^-105, and the other groups' terms were off by the rounding: 20% on a gate file of groups at very different scales). `B` is therefore computed as `B = (1 / W) sum over the pairs i < j of w_i w_j (m_i - m_j)^2`, where **every term is a product of positive numbers** and each difference of two group means is itself an exact rational `(S1_i n_j - S1_j n_i) / (n_i n_j)` (a big-integer subtraction of exact sums, then one division to double-double): nothing cancels whatever the weights and the offsets. The cost is `k (k - 1) / 2` such divisions (measured in section 9). Everything is scaled by powers of two so that the largest weight and the largest difference are near 2^60 and unscaled once at the end; a nonzero weight or difference more than 2^360 below the largest makes the answer `null` with **`reason: "welch-range"`** (a new reason: the variances or the distances between the means span more than 100 decimal digits), never a wrong number. Welch's `F*` and `df2` are correct to about 2^-100 relative to the largest term of the sum, so they are the correctly rounded doubles except within that distance of a tie (`rounding` is not printed: the answer does not carry a flag for it; the gates count the cases that are not the correctly rounded value). Null with a reason: fewer than two groups with a value, or a classic `N - k < 1`: `too-few-rows` (for Welch also any group of one value; the effect sizes stand); a group without variance (Welch), or `SSW = 0` (classic): `variance-zero`.
* **`kruskal`** (built in S4.2, section 10). With `R_i` the rank sum of group `i`: `H = 12 / (N (N + 1)) sum R_i^2 / n_i - 3 (N + 1)`, corrected by `C = 1 - sum (t_j^3 - t_j) / (N^3 - N)`, `H* = H / C` (exact rational, one rounding; `C = 0`, every value equal: `variance-zero`). Effect size `epsilon^2 = H* / (N - 1)`. `p_method: "chi2"`. **As built:** the doubled rank sums `D_i = 2 R_i` and the tie sizes come from the sorted exact order keys; with the fraction `Sn / Dn = sum D_i^2 / n_i` folded as in `anova` (`Dn = prod n_i`), `X = Sn - N (N + 1)^2 Dn` (never negative: Cauchy-Schwarz), `E = N^3 - N - sum (t^3 - t)`: `H* = 3 (N - 1) X / (Dn E)` and `epsilon^2 = 3 X / (Dn E)`, each one rounding; `E = 0` is `variance-zero`; fewer than two groups with a value is `too-few-rows`. The answer also lists the mean rank of each group (`D_i / (2 n_i)`, one rounding) and the number of tie groups. The file is read once per group and only the chosen columns' order keys are kept (`N` times the columns, bounded by `--max-cells`: `limit.too-many-cells`).
* **`mannwhitney`** (built in S4.3, section 11). With `R_a` the rank sum of group `a`: `U_a = R_a - n_a (n_a + 1) / 2` (a half-integer only through ties: `2 U_a` is an integer), `U_b = n_a n_b - U_a`. The effect size is the rank-biserial correlation `r = (U_a - U_b) / (n_a n_b)` (exact, one rounding; the sign says that `a` tends to be larger when positive). **Asymptotic:** `z = (U_a - n_a n_b / 2 - c) / sigma` with `sigma^2 = n_a n_b / 12 ((N + 1) - sum (t_j^3 - t_j) / (N (N - 1)))` and the continuity correction `c = 0.5 sign(U_a - n_a n_b / 2)` (the default of R and scipy; `--continuity none` removes it), `p = 2 norm_sf(|z|)`; `z` is one rounding of an exact rational (a square root, as `z` of `prop`). **Exact:** the distribution of `U` without ties by counting (dynamic programming over the subsets, integers below `C(60, 30) < 2^63`), `p = min(1, 2 min(P(U <= u), P(U >= u)))`, an exact rational over `C(N, n_a)` rounded once.
* **`wilcoxon`** (built in S4.4, section 12). The differences `d_i = a_i - b_i` (paired) or `x_i - mu0`, exact (a difference of two doubles is an exact integer in the unit 2^-1074); zeros are dropped (`--zeros wilcox`, the default of R and scipy; `pratt` keeps them in the ranking and drops them from the sum, `zsplit` splits their ranks), `n` is the number of non-zero differences; the ranks are the average ranks of `|d_i|`; `W+` is the sum of the ranks of the positive ones, `W-` of the negative ones. The effect size is the matched-pairs rank-biserial correlation `(W+ - W-) / (W+ + W-)` (exact, one rounding). **Asymptotic:** `z = (W+ - n (n + 1) / 4 - c) / sigma`, `sigma^2 = n (n + 1) (2 n + 1) / 24 - sum (t_j^3 - t_j) / 48`, as above. **Exact:** the counts of the subsets of `{1, .., n}` by sum (integers below `2^50`), `p = min(1, 2 min(P(W+ <= w), P(W+ >= w)))`.
* **`ks`.** `D = d / (n_a n_b)` with `d` the largest integer distance found by merging the sorted order keys (ties across groups are stepped together), so `D` is one rounding of an exact rational. **Exact** (no two values equal, in the pooled sample or within a group, and `N <= 60`; *corrected in place in S4.5: this said "no ties across the groups", but a tie inside one group also changes the conditional distribution of the arrangements, which the count assumes uniform over distinct values*): the number of lattice paths that stay inside the band `|i n_b - j n_a| < d`, `p = 1 - paths / C(N, n_a)` = P(D >= D_obs), an exact rational; **asymptotic** (otherwise): `p = Q_KS(lambda)` with `lambda = sqrt(n_a n_b / N) D`, `Q_KS(l) = 2 sum_{j >= 1} (-1)^(j - 1) exp(-2 j^2 l^2)`, which needs a new special function (`special.dist`, gated against `mpmath` first, as every function of S0 was). No Stephens correction (stated in `p_method`). One-sample `ks` against a distribution is **not** in v1 (a closed list of distributions with parameters is `fit-dist`'s business).
* **`fisher`.** For the 2 x 2 table `(x, r1 - x; c1 - x, r2 - c1 + x)` with margins `r1, r2, c1, c2`, the probability of `x` is `C(r1, x) C(r2, c1 - x) / C(N, c1)`. The **two-sided p-value is the exact sum of the probabilities not larger than the observed one**: the comparison is of two exact integers (`acc.bigint`), so there is **no tolerance** (R and scipy use a relative `1 + 1e-7`, which can include a table that is larger by less than that; the answer of this tool is the definition, and it says so: `p_method: "exact, probabilities not larger than the observed"`). The statistic is the sample odds ratio `x11 x22 / (x12 x21)` (exact, one rounding; `null` with `reason: "odds-ratio-undefined"` when a cell product is 0 in both numerator and denominator, `Infinity` is not a JSON number: also `null` with `reason: "odds-ratio-infinite"`). Effect size: the odds ratio itself; no interval in v1 (the conditional maximum-likelihood estimate and its exact interval need a root-finder on the noncentral hypergeometric distribution: **[open]**, after S5). Refused above `N = 10,000` (`limit.exact-too-large`, repair: `chisq`).

## 3. Exactness, determinism **[choice]**

The statistics of `kruskal`, `mannwhitney`, `wilcoxon`, `ks`, `fisher` and the classic `anova` are one rounding of exact rationals of integers (bytes against `Fraction`). `anova`'s Welch statistic is the one double-double case (the `chisq` precedent). The exact p-values are exact rationals rounded once; the asymptotic ones are as accurate as `special.dist` (2e-13 for the normal and chi-square, 1e-12 for `F`, **[read]** `docs/numerics.md` section 3.2) plus the amplification of the rounding of the statistic. No `conc`, no clock; ranks and order keys depend on the values only, so a shuffle of the rows cannot change a bit.

## 4. The exact tests and their limits **[choice]**

**No silent approximations** (`docs/design.md` section 5). `--exact auto|yes|no` (default `auto`) for `mannwhitney`, `wilcoxon` and `ks`: `auto` uses the exact p-value when there are no ties (any two values equal, for `ks`) and `N <= 60` (`mannwhitney`, `ks`) or `n <= 50` (`wilcoxon`), else the asymptotic one, and **the answer says which** (`p_method`); `yes` with ties or above the limit is refused (`stats.exact-unavailable`, the detail names which, the repair is `--exact no`); `no` always uses the asymptotic p-value. The limits are the sizes at which the counts fit a 64-bit integer; beyond them the asymptotic p-value is the answer. `fisher` has no asymptotic mode; above its limit the repair names `chisq`.

## 6. Output, refusals

New rule tags **[choice]**: `stats.exact-unavailable` (`--exact yes` with ties or above the limit), `limit.exact-too-large` (`fisher` above `N = 10,000`); `stats.group-levels` is reused (`anova` and `kruskal` need `k >= 2` levels and at most `--max-groups`; `mannwhitney` and `ks` exactly two; `fisher` two levels in each column). A statistic that is undefined is `null` with a `reason` (`too-few-rows`, `variance-zero`, `odds-ratio-undefined`, `odds-ratio-infinite`, and for `anova` Welch's `welch-range`), never a refusal. The new flags: `--exact auto|yes|no`, `--continuity auto|none`, `--zeros wilcox|pratt|zsplit`, and (reused) `--variance welch|pooled` for `anova`.

## 8. Decisions asked for, and what is proposed

The proposals stand unless overturned; each is a **[choice]**.

1. `anova` defaults to Welch's `F*` (`--variance pooled` for the classic one), as `t2` does. Alternative: the classic `F` by default, which is what `f_oneway` and most textbooks mean by "ANOVA".
2. The exact tests are used by default when there are no ties and the size allows (`--exact auto`), and the answer says so. Alternative: asymptotic by default, exact on request.
3. `fisher`'s two-sided p-value is the exact definition (probabilities not larger than the observed one) without the `1e-7` tolerance of R and scipy; the difference is documented and measured.
4. `ks` is two-sample only in v1; no Stephens correction.
5. `fisher` has no confidence interval in v1.
6. No post-hoc pairwise comparisons after `anova`/`kruskal` (Tukey, Dunn) in v1: a family of `mannwhitney` or `t2` tests with `--correct` is the available route; they are a separate design if wanted.
7. `corr --exact` (exact permutation p-values for Spearman and Kendall) is S4.7 and optional.

## 9. S4.1 `anova`: what was built and measured **[measured]**

Machine: 4 cores, no GPU, `build/stats` at the commit of this slice. Scripts: `scripts/gate_anova.py` (R-A, R-B, R-C, R-F, R-G with the oracle `ot.anova`, which shares no code with the program), `scripts/gate_anova_calibration.py` (R-D and the power), `scripts/mutants_anova.py` (R-H), the table and fuzz of `scripts/gate_describe_refusals.py`, and the lines `test-anova` of `docs/xplat_fp.txt`.

* **Exact statistics and p-values (seed 2, 1,500 files, 1,890 tests, 1,013 answered).** n, mean and sd of every group, `F`, `df`, `eta^2`, `omega^2` and the reasons: every byte equal to the oracle. **Welch's `F*`: 324 compared, 0 not the correctly rounded double.** p-values against `mpmath` at the exact statistic: largest relative error 8.4e-14, 0.8% of the bound `1e-11 + (d1 + d2 + 1) 2^-53`. Controls outside the bound: chi-square approximation 1013 of 1013, `d2 + 1` 1013 of 1013, swapped degrees of freedom 653 of 653, the other variance assumption 655 of 655. Ten Welch tests were `welch-range` (the oracle predicts the status from the bit lengths of the same integers; between 352 and 368 bits either answer is accepted, above 368 the status is required). Fixed hand cases include variances 2^418 apart, means 2^637 apart at equal variances, `F*` near 1e32, a pooled `F` beyond the largest double, a group of one value, constant groups, huge and subnormal values, a group with no value.
* **scipy** `f_oneway` (classic `F`, 318 well-conditioned columns): relative difference of the statistic at most 2.1e-10 (at `F = 7e4`, where scipy's own sums lose digits; bound `1e-10 + 1e-14 F`, set after seeing it, said here), of the p-value 1.1e-10.
* **Invariance.** The rows shuffled, `--cols` in another order: the same bytes on every file. The levels renamed so that their byte order reverses: the same statistic, p-value and effect sizes (Welch's within 2 units) and the per-group lists reversed. `--correct` (holm, bh, bonferroni) matches the oracle's adjusted values on a random third of the files.
* **Calibration** (4,000 replicates per configuration, seed 1). Classic `F` with equal variances (sizes down to 2 per group): inside the 99.9% binomial interval and uniform (KS p > 0.001) on all 6 configurations; with unequal variances and sizes it is outside, as it must be (e.g. 1,210 rejections of 4,000 at sizes 10..90, sds 5..1). Welch's `F*` is inside on every configuration with 10 or more values per group (e.g. 217 and 211 of 4,000 at sizes (10,20,40) and (40,20,10) with sds (3,2,1)); below 10 it is printed, not gated (two values per group: 127 of 4,000, conservative). Power with means (0, 0.5, 1) at sizes (15,20,25): noncentral `F` (scipy) 0.774972, observed 3,129 of 4,000 (interval 3,012..3,186).
* **Refusals.** 265 table cases (28 rule tags) and 1,000 fuzz files: no trap, no hang, every refusal tagged; includes groups times columns exactly at the limit 8,192 (answered).
* **Mutants.** 88: 80 killed, 8 survive and each is explained in the script (index bound whose top limb is always zero; scale choices that powers of two make exact; a global sign; a zero-difference pair; a field the entry nulls anyway; a Welch overflow that the 2^360 range makes unreachable, an **argument, not a measurement**). 3 mutants of the first draft were removed with the dead code they mutated (`low` in the double-double conversion).
* **Time** (one column, 4 cores idle): 50 groups of 20 rows 0.35 s; 200 groups of 10 rows 1.5 s; 1,000 groups of 5 rows 16 s (the file is read once per group, and Welch's pairs are `k (k - 1) / 2` divisions).

Findings that changed the design are in place in section 2 (the pairwise form of `B`, `welch-range`, `k` passes, the 8,192 bound). Open: a fast path for large `k` (one pass for all groups) if it is wanted; the default of `--max-groups` stays 50.

## 10. S4.2 `kruskal`: what was built and measured **[measured]**

Machine: 4 cores, no GPU. Scripts: `scripts/gate_kruskal.py` (oracle `ot.kruskal`: ranks `Fraction`s by comparing them, sharing no code with the program), `scripts/gate_kruskal_calibration.py`, `scripts/mutants_kruskal.py`, the kruskal cases of `scripts/gate_describe_refusals.py`, the lines `test-kruskal` of `docs/xplat_fp.txt`.

* **Exact statistics and p-values (seed 2, 1,000 files, 925 run, 1,260 tests, 1,166 answered).** n and mean rank of every group, the number of tie groups, `H*`, `epsilon^2`, `df` and the reasons: every byte equal to the oracle. p-values against `mpmath` at the exact statistic: largest relative error 1.8e-14 (0.2% of the bound). Controls outside the bound: chi-square on `k` degrees of freedom 1149 of 1149, on `k - 2` 1015 of 1015, `H` without the tie correction 331 of 331 (where there are ties), the one-sided p-value 1149 of 1149. scipy `kruskal` on 632 well-conditioned columns: relative difference of the statistic at most 1.6e-13, of the p-value 2.0e-13.
* **Invariance.** The rows shuffled, `--cols` in another order, the values times 8 and the values negated (a monotone map, and its reverse): the same bytes of statistic, p-value, effect size, df, ties and n; the levels renamed so that their byte order reverses: the same answer with the lists reversed. `--correct` matches the oracle's adjusted values.

## 12. S4.4 `wilcoxon`: what was built and measured **[measured]**

`stats test wilcoxon (--pairs A:B,.. | --cols A,.. [--mu0 X]) [--zeros wilcox|pratt|zsplit] [--exact auto|yes|no] [--continuity auto|none]`; exactly one of `--pairs` and `--cols` (else `stats.test-columns`). **As built:**

* **The differences are exact and ranked exactly.** `d = a - b` (or `x - mu0`) of two doubles is not a double; Knuth's two-sum gives `s = fl(a - b)` and the rounding error `e` with `d = s + e`, no bit lost. Rounding is monotone, so `|d|` is ordered by the pair `(|s|, sign(s) e)`, two order keys compared one after the other (a merge sort of indices on the pair), and two differences are tied exactly when both keys are equal. **[choice, stated because it differs from scipy]** scipy subtracts in floating point: `3 - 2.6e-302` and `3 - 1.1e-317` are both `3.0` for it, a tie that the exact differences do not have; this tool ranks the exact ones. The two agree where every difference is exact in floating point (the gate compares with scipy only there). A difference beyond the largest double (`1e308 - -1e308`) makes the test `null` with `reason: "difference-beyond-double"`.
* **The three methods for zeros** (the doubled average ranks are integers, so each statistic is exact): `wilcox` drops the zero differences before ranking; `pratt` ranks them with the others (they take the lowest ranks) and drops them from the sums, with mean `(N (N + 1) - Z (Z + 1)) / 4` and variance `[N (N + 1) (2 N + 1) - Z (Z + 1) (2 Z + 1)] / 24 - T / 48` over the ties of the nonzero differences; `zsplit` ranks them and shares their ranks between `W+` and `W-` (mean `N (N + 1) / 4`, ties counted including the zeros). `W+`, `W-` and the rank-biserial correlation `(W+ - W-) / (W+ + W-)` are exact. With only zero differences `W+ = W- = 0` is answered, the score and the p-value are `null`, `reason: "variance-zero"` (for `zsplit` the score is 0 and the p-value 1: the definition gives a positive variance).
* **p-values.** Exact (the count of subsets of the ranks `1 .. n` by sum, over `2^n`: no ties, no zero ranked, `1 <= n <= 50` nonzero differences, counts below 2^50) as an exact rational rounded once; else the normal score `z^2 = 12 (|2 W+ - mean2| - c)^2 / (2 S - T3)` (one rounding through an exact integer square root), tie-corrected, with the continuity correction unless `--continuity none`. `--exact yes` where the exact p-value is not available is `stats.exact-unavailable` (the tag of S4.3, its message names the cases).
* **Gates** `scripts/gate_wilcoxon.py` (oracle `ot.wilcoxon`: exact `Fraction` differences, ranks by comparison, counts by the product of `(1 + q^i)` checked against the enumeration of every subset up to 8), `scripts/gate_wilcoxon_calibration.py`, `scripts/mutants_wilcoxon.py`, the wilcoxon cases of the refusals gate, `test-wilcoxon` in `docs/xplat_fp.txt`.

**Measured.** (Seed 3, 1,000 files, 881 run, 881 tests, 844 answered, 116 `--exact yes` refusals expected and seen; the hand cases run under all three methods for zeros and all three exact modes.) Every number equal to the oracle: counts of rows, nonzero differences and zeros, ties, `W+`, `W-`, rank-biserial correlation, `z` (bytes), the reasons. **Exact p-values: 368 of 368 equal to the oracle's rational rounded once, bit for bit.** Normal p-values against `mpmath` at the exact `z^2`: largest relative error 1.0e-14. Controls outside the bound: the normal p without the continuity correction 462 of 476, the normal p where the exact one is the answer 305 of 368, the p of another method for zeros 116 of 126, the one-sided p 844 of 844. scipy `wilcoxon` on 415 tests whose differences are exact in floating point, all three methods for zeros: statistic equal, exact p equal, normal p within 2.8e-14 (so `pratt` and `zsplit` agree with scipy's, which this document had not assumed: the definitions above were written from the textbook and then compared). Invariance: the rows shuffled, the values times 8 (same bytes); the pair named the other way round: `W+` and `W-` exchange, `z` and the rank-biserial correlation change sign, the p-value is the same bytes. No control for the tie correction (unlike `mannwhitney`): not built, said here.

* **Calibration** (2,000 replicates, seed 1). The exact test is judged against its exact size (0.00000 at n = 5: the smallest two-sided p is 2/32 = 0.0625, so it never rejects at 0.05 and the interval is 0..0; 0.03906 at 8, 0.04248 at 12, 0.04844 at 20, 0.04946 at 35, 0.04945 at 50): all inside. The normal approximation (n = 51, 100, 300; KS p above 0.001) and the symmetric integer lattice with zeros and ties (n = 40, 100, for each of the three methods for zeros) are inside the interval around 0.05. Refused: the normal p without the continuity correction against the exact size, the one-sided p, 0.05 as the size of an exact test. Power with a shift of 0.5 sd at n = 30: 1,504 of 2,000, printed.
* **Refusals.** 348 table cases (29 rule tags) and 1,000 fuzz files: no trap, no hang, every refusal tagged.
* **Mutants.** 75: 71 killed, 4 explained in the script (two equivalent code; two resting on the argument, not measured, that a zero variance comes only with no nonzero difference). Six of the 75 were first found alive and killed by adding gate cases (a negative overflow, only zero differences under each method, the names of the test and the absence of `level`, `--exact yes` with a zero variance).

## 13. S4.5 `ks`: what was built and measured **[measured]**

`stats test ks --cols A,B --group G [--exact auto|yes|no]`: exactly two levels (`stats.group-levels` otherwise), group `a` the level first by bytes; no `--continuity` (the flag does not apply). **As built:** the present values of a column with the index of their group are sorted by their exact order keys (`wilcoxon.sort_idx`, shared); walking the sorted values with the values of equal key stepped together, `i` and `j` count the values of `a` and of `b` seen, `d = max |i n_b - j n_a|` is an integer, and `D = d / (n_a n_b)` is one rounding of a quotient of two integers below 2^53 (`statistic`; `distance` prints `d`). `lambda^2 = d^2 / (N n_a n_b)` is an exact rational and `lambda` its root through `central.sqrt_ratio_bits` (one rounding). **Exact p** (no two values equal, `N <= 60`, `stest.exact.ks_paths`): a dynamic program over the `(n_a + 1) x (n_b + 1)` grid counts the paths that stay strictly inside the band `|i n_b - j n_a| < d` and all paths (`C(N, n_a) <= C(60, 30) = 1.18e17 < 2^63`); `p = (total - inside) / total` = P(D >= D_obs), an exact rational rounded once; `d = 0` has `p = 1`. **Asymptotic p** otherwise: `Q_KS(lambda)` by `special.dist.ks_sf` (`docs/numerics.md` section 3.1, gated against `mpmath` before it was used), no Stephens correction; `p_method` is `"exact"` or `"asymptotic"`. `--exact yes` where the exact p-value is not available is `stats.exact-unavailable` (a property of the pooled values of the column, whether or not a group is empty: the gate found, at file 271, that the driver refuses a column with ties whose second group is empty, and the gate of `mannwhitney` now expects the same). A group with no value makes the test `null`, `reason: "too-few-rows"`. `ties` is the number of groups of equal values over both groups.

* **scipy.** `ks_2samp(method="exact")` agrees with this tool on `D` (largest relative difference 2.78e-16) and on the exact p-value (4.28e-16) on 83 well-conditioned columns of the gate (`--scipy`, 300 files). **scipy's asymptotic p-value is not compared and does not match**: it evaluates the Kolmogorov distribution of the `round(en)`-sample statistic (`kstwo`), a different approximation from `Q_KS(lambda)`; this tool prints `p_method: "asymptotic"` and the value of `Q_KS` at the exact `lambda`, measured against `mpmath` only (below).
* **Statistics and p-values** (`scripts/gate_ks.py`, oracle `ot.ks`: `D` from the two distribution functions at every distinct value as `Fraction`s, no pooled sort; the exact count by a recurrence that counts the paths that have LEFT the band, checked against the enumeration of every choice for every size up to 5 x 5; the asymptotic p-value from the alternating series at 160 digits at the exact `lambda`). 300 files, 246 run with 334 tests compared, 28 refusals expected and seen, 0 differ; the exact p-values equal the oracle's rational rounded once, bit for bit, 190 of 190; the asymptotic ones against `mpmath` have a largest relative error 6.71e-15 over 142 (bound `1e-11 + 2^-53`). Invariance (shuffled rows, `--cols` in another order, values times 8, negated, levels renamed so that their byte order flips with the sizes swapped): the same bytes.
* **Calibration** (`scripts/gate_ks_calibration.py`, 2,000 replicates, seed 1, run on a 6-worker machine in 3.5 minutes). Neither rule has size 0.05, and both are judged against their **exact size**, counted by the same recurrence (the rule rejects when `d` is at least the smallest `d` whose p-value is below 0.05): (5,5) 0.00794, (8,12) 0.03199, (10,10) 0.01234, (20,20) 0.03354, (30,30) 0.03458 for the exact p, (31,30) 0.03243, (100,100) 0.03638, (100,200) 0.04063, (300,300) 0.04234 for the asymptotic one: **the asymptotic Kolmogorov test is conservative at every size measured and approaches 0.05 slowly** (stated; there is no correction to improve it in v1). Every configuration is inside the 99.9% interval around its exact size (e.g. (300,300): 83 rejections of 2,000, interval 57 to 116). ## 14. S4.6 `fisher`: what was built and measured **[measured]**

`stats test fisher --cols A,B,.. --vs G [--correct holm|bh|bonferroni]`: each column named by `--cols` against `--vs`, each with exactly two levels among the rows where both cells are present (`stats.group-levels` otherwise, for one level or for three; a column with no complete row is `null`, `reason: "too-few-rows"`, like `chisq`). The table `(a b; c d)` has the first level of the column (by bytes) as its first row and the first level of `--vs` as its first column; it is printed in `assumptions.table` with the levels. **As built** (`stest.fisher`): the weights `w(x) = C(r1, x) C(r2, c1 - x)` are walked from the first possible `x` by `w(x + 1) = w(x) (r1 - x)(c1 - x) / ((x + 1)(r2 - c1 + x + 1))` in four exact steps (multiply by `r1 - x`, divide by `x + 1`, multiply by `c1 - x`, divide by `r2 - c1 + x + 1`; each division is exact because its quotient is the next binomial), on big integers of 26-bit limbs with a multiplication and a division by a small number (the shift-and-subtract division of `acc.bigint` would cost about 4 x 10^10 limb operations at the largest size, an estimate from its loop counts, not measured, and is not used). Two passes: the first keeps `w(a)`; the second adds every `w` to `W` and those with `w <= w(a)` to the numerator, **compared as integers, with no tolerance**; `p = numerator / W` is rounded once (`central.ratio_bits`). `W = C(N, c1)` is the sum, not computed apart. The odds ratio `a d / (b c)` is one division of two exact integers below 2^53 (one rounding), also the effect size (`odds_ratio`); `b c = 0 < a d` is `null` with `reason: "odds-ratio-infinite"`, and the p-value is answered all the same. `odds-ratio-undefined` (`a d = b c = 0`) cannot be reached from the command line: with two levels in each column no cell product pair can both vanish (the argument: `a d = 0` and `b c = 0` empty a row or a column), so it is the path of `fisher.run` only. No interval (`interval: null`; the conditional maximum-likelihood estimate and its interval need a root-finder: open, after S5). `p_method` is `"exact, probabilities not larger than the observed"`. **`limit.exact-too-large`** (exit 8, repair: `chisq`) **[new rule tag]** above 10,000 complete rows; at 10,000 the whole answer takes 0.07 s on the 4-core machine of the gates (measured once, load of the mutants running).

* **The tolerance.** R and scipy add a table to the sum when its probability is at most `(1 + 1e-7)` times the observed one. The gate holds three tables found by search (`(50,79,159,202)` with N = 490, where the table with `a = 60` is larger by a relative 1.1e-8; `(131,601,169,598)` with N = 1,499, 1.5e-8; `(12,316,96,639)` with N = 1,063, 4.9e-8): on each, the p-value with the tolerance **differs** from this tool's, which is the definition (the control `tolerance-1e-7` is outside the bound on 3 of 3). The two agree on every other table of the gate; scipy's `fisher_exact` is therefore compared only where the oracle's two sums are equal.
* **Statistics and p-values** (`scripts/gate_fisher.py`, oracle `ot.fisher`: `math.comb` weights as `Fraction`s, checked against the enumeration of every choice of the first column for every table of up to 12 items). 300 files (293 run, 522 tests compared, 7 refusals expected and seen), 0 differ; the p-values equal the oracle's rational rounded once, bit for bit, 521 of 521. Controls outside the bound: twice the smaller tail 322 of 426, the one-sided tail 400 of 426, the tolerance 3 of 3. `fisher_exact` agrees on the odds ratio exactly (0) and on the p-value to a relative 1.7e-15 on 273 tables. Invariance (rows shuffled, `--cols` in another order, labels renamed so that their byte order flips: table rows swapped and the same p-value, the two columns exchanged: the same odds ratio and p-value): the same numbers.
* **No calibration gate.** The exact test is conditional on the margins and its p-value is the oracle's rational at every table, which the gate already checks; a null simulation would compare the same sums with their own sampling noise.
* **Refusals.** 394 table cases (30 rule tags) and 1,000 fuzz files: no trap, no hang, every refusal tagged.
* **Mutants.** `scripts/mutants_fisher.py`: 42, 38 killed and 4 explained in the script as equivalent (the binomial's symmetry only changes the step count; the two first-weight branches agree at `c1 = r2`; beyond `min(r1, c1)` every weight is 0; limbs above the length in use are never read).
