# `stats test`: the rank, variance and exact tests (milestone S4)

**Status: design only, written 2026-10-24 before any code of S4. Nothing here has been measured; what it reads from the code of S0 to S3 is marked [read], every bound is a [choice] fixed before the first run and corrected in place, in this document, if a run shows it false.** Tags: **[read]** read from a document or source, **[measured]** printed by a script (none yet), **[choice]** a decision the maintainer can overturn, **[open]** not decided. S4 is the rest of the table of `docs/design.md` section 4: `anova`, `kruskal`, `mannwhitney`, `wilcoxon`, `ks` and `fisher`. The conventions of `docs/test.md` (the answer, the flags `--cols`, `--group`, `--pairs`, `--level`, `--correct`, `--max-tests`, the rule tags, the gates and the discipline of the mutants and the fingerprint) are not repeated; this document says what is new. Issue #5.

## 1. What it adds

`stats test KIND FILE` with six more kinds. The answer is the one of `docs/test.md` section 1 (`statistic`, `df`, `p_value`, `p_method`, `effect_size`, `interval`, `n`, `method`, `assumptions`, `reason`); `p_method` says which of the exact and the asymptotic p-value was used, and `--correct` works over any of them.

| kind | question | inputs | statistic | p-value |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `anova` | do `k >= 2` groups have the same mean? | `--cols A,B,..` `--group G` (`k` levels, at most `--max-groups`), `--variance welch\|pooled` | `F` (Welch's `F*` by default, the classic `F` with `pooled`) | `f_sf` |
| `kruskal` | do `k >= 2` groups have the same distribution (location)? | as `anova` | `H`, tie-corrected | `chi2_sf` on `k - 1` |
| `mannwhitney` | does a value of group `a` tend to exceed one of group `b`? | `--cols A,B,..` `--group G` (two levels) | `U` of group `a` | exact, or normal with tie and continuity correction |
| `wilcoxon` | is the median of the differences zero? | `--pairs A:B,..` or `--cols A,.. --mu0 m` | `W+`, the sum of the ranks of the positive differences | exact, or normal with tie and continuity correction |
| `ks` | do two groups have the same distribution? | `--cols A,B,..` `--group G` (two levels) | `D`, the largest distance of the two empirical distribution functions | exact, or the Kolmogorov distribution |
| `fisher` | are two binary columns independent? (2 x 2) | `--cols A --vs G`, each with exactly two levels | the sample odds ratio | exact: the hypergeometric probabilities of the tables as or less likely than the observed one |

## 2. What each computes **[choice]**

**Ranks and ties.** Ranks are the average ranks of `lib/corr/ranks.cho` **[read]**; a rank sum is kept as the integer `2 R` (doubled ranks), so every statistic below is an exact rational of integers and is rounded once. The size of each tie group `t_j` is counted from the sorted order keys, which are exact (`rank.key_of`, **[read]**). Missing cells are dropped as in S3, and the answer says how many (`n`).

* **`anova`.** With `n_i`, `S1_i`, `S2_i` per group (the exact power sums of `describe`, **[read]**), `N`, `S1`, the classic quantities are the exact integers `SSB = sum S1_i^2 / n_i - S1^2 / N` and `SSW = sum S2_i - sum S1_i^2 / n_i`; `F = (SSB / (k - 1)) / (SSW / (N - k))` on `(k - 1, N - k)` degrees of freedom is **one rounding of an exact rational** (denominators below `prod n_i`, built with `acc.bigint`). The effect sizes `eta^2 = SSB / (SSB + SSW)` and `omega^2 = (SSB - (k - 1) MSW) / (SST + MSW)` likewise. **Welch's `F*`** (the default, as Welch is the default of `t2`; unequal variances are the usual case) has weights `w_i = n_i / s_i^2 = n_i^2 (n_i - 1) / L_i` whose exact sum has a common denominator `prod L_i` of thousands of bits per group: **[choice]** its terms are formed as exact rationals and summed in double-double, the result is a correctly rounded double except within about 2^-100 of a tie (the same device and the same label, `rounding: "double-double"`, as `chisq`), and `df2` likewise. Refused-as-null: a group of one value (no variance) makes Welch's weights undefined: `reason: "too-few-rows"`; all groups constant: `variance-zero`.
* **`kruskal`.** With `R_i` the rank sum of group `i`: `H = 12 / (N (N + 1)) sum R_i^2 / n_i - 3 (N + 1)`, corrected by `C = 1 - sum (t_j^3 - t_j) / (N^3 - N)`, `H* = H / C` (exact rational, one rounding; `C = 0`, every value equal: `variance-zero`). Effect size `epsilon^2 = H* / (N - 1)`. `p_method: "chi2"`.
* **`mannwhitney`.** With `R_a` the rank sum of group `a`: `U_a = R_a - n_a (n_a + 1) / 2` (a half-integer only through ties: `2 U_a` is an integer), `U_b = n_a n_b - U_a`. The effect size is the rank-biserial correlation `r = (U_a - U_b) / (n_a n_b)` (exact, one rounding; the sign says that `a` tends to be larger when positive). **Asymptotic:** `z = (U_a - n_a n_b / 2 - c) / sigma` with `sigma^2 = n_a n_b / 12 ((N + 1) - sum (t_j^3 - t_j) / (N (N - 1)))` and the continuity correction `c = 0.5 sign(U_a - n_a n_b / 2)` (the default of R and scipy; `--continuity none` removes it), `p = 2 norm_sf(|z|)`; `z` is one rounding of an exact rational (a square root, as `z` of `prop`). **Exact:** the distribution of `U` without ties by counting (dynamic programming over the subsets, integers below `C(60, 30) < 2^63`), `p = min(1, 2 min(P(U <= u), P(U >= u)))`, an exact rational over `C(N, n_a)` rounded once.
* **`wilcoxon`.** The differences `d_i = a_i - b_i` (paired) or `x_i - mu0`, exact (a difference of two doubles is an exact integer in the unit 2^-1074); zeros are dropped (`--zeros wilcox`, the default of R and scipy; `pratt` keeps them in the ranking and drops them from the sum, `zsplit` splits their ranks), `n` is the number of non-zero differences; the ranks are the average ranks of `|d_i|`; `W+` is the sum of the ranks of the positive ones, `W-` of the negative ones. The effect size is the matched-pairs rank-biserial correlation `(W+ - W-) / (W+ + W-)` (exact, one rounding). **Asymptotic:** `z = (W+ - n (n + 1) / 4 - c) / sigma`, `sigma^2 = n (n + 1) (2 n + 1) / 24 - sum (t_j^3 - t_j) / 48`, as above. **Exact:** the counts of the subsets of `{1, .., n}` by sum (integers below `2^50`), `p = min(1, 2 min(P(W+ <= w), P(W+ >= w)))`.
* **`ks`.** `D = d / (n_a n_b)` with `d` the largest integer distance found by merging the sorted order keys (ties across groups are stepped together), so `D` is one rounding of an exact rational. **Exact** (no ties across the groups, `N <= 60`): the number of lattice paths that stay inside the band `|i / n_a - j / n_b| < D`, `p = 1 - paths / C(N, n_a)`, an exact rational; **asymptotic** (otherwise): `p = Q_KS(lambda)` with `lambda = sqrt(n_a n_b / N) D`, `Q_KS(l) = 2 sum_{j >= 1} (-1)^(j - 1) exp(-2 j^2 l^2)`, which needs a new special function (`special.dist`, gated against `mpmath` first, as every function of S0 was). No Stephens correction (stated in `p_method`). One-sample `ks` against a distribution is **not** in v1 (a closed list of distributions with parameters is `fit-dist`'s business).
* **`fisher`.** For the 2 x 2 table `(x, r1 - x; c1 - x, r2 - c1 + x)` with margins `r1, r2, c1, c2`, the probability of `x` is `C(r1, x) C(r2, c1 - x) / C(N, c1)`. The **two-sided p-value is the exact sum of the probabilities not larger than the observed one**: the comparison is of two exact integers (`acc.bigint`), so there is **no tolerance** (R and scipy use a relative `1 + 1e-7`, which can include a table that is larger by less than that; the answer of this tool is the definition, and it says so: `p_method: "exact, probabilities not larger than the observed"`). The statistic is the sample odds ratio `x11 x22 / (x12 x21)` (exact, one rounding; `null` with `reason: "odds-ratio-undefined"` when a cell product is 0 in both numerator and denominator, `Infinity` is not a JSON number: also `null` with `reason: "odds-ratio-infinite"`). Effect size: the odds ratio itself; no interval in v1 (the conditional maximum-likelihood estimate and its exact interval need a root-finder on the noncentral hypergeometric distribution: **[open]**, after S5). Refused above `N = 10,000` (`limit.exact-too-large`, repair: `chisq`).

## 3. Exactness, determinism **[choice]**

The statistics of `kruskal`, `mannwhitney`, `wilcoxon`, `ks`, `fisher` and the classic `anova` are one rounding of exact rationals of integers (bytes against `Fraction`). `anova`'s Welch statistic is the one double-double case (the `chisq` precedent). The exact p-values are exact rationals rounded once; the asymptotic ones are as accurate as `special.dist` (2e-13 for the normal and chi-square, 1e-12 for `F`, **[read]** `docs/numerics.md` section 3.2) plus the amplification of the rounding of the statistic. No `conc`, no clock; ranks and order keys depend on the values only, so a shuffle of the rows cannot change a bit.

## 4. The exact tests and their limits **[choice]**

**No silent approximations** (`docs/design.md` section 5). `--exact auto|yes|no` (default `auto`) for `mannwhitney`, `wilcoxon` and `ks`: `auto` uses the exact p-value when there are no ties (across groups for `ks`) and `N <= 60` (`mannwhitney`, `ks`) or `n <= 50` (`wilcoxon`), else the asymptotic one, and **the answer says which** (`p_method`); `yes` with ties or above the limit is refused (`stats.exact-unavailable`, the detail names which, the repair is `--exact no`); `no` always uses the asymptotic p-value. The limits are the sizes at which the counts fit a 64-bit integer; beyond them the asymptotic p-value is the answer. `fisher` has no asymptotic mode; above its limit the repair names `chisq`.

| id | gate | fails when |
|---|---|---|
| R-A | **Exact statistics** against `Fraction` formulas written independently (the oracle ranks by sorting `Fraction`s, counts ties itself, and enumerates the subsets or the tables by brute force for small sizes), bytes compared: `F`, `eta^2`, `omega^2`, `H`, `U`, the rank-biserial correlations, `W+`, `D`, the odds ratio, `n` per group, the tie counts; families of files with ties of every size, constants, groups of size 1, 2, missing cells, offsets and wide exponents | one byte differs |
| R-B | **p-values**: the exact ones (`mannwhitney`, `wilcoxon`, `ks`, `fisher`) equal the exact rational of the oracle **bit for bit** (the oracle enumerates every arrangement for `N <= 14` and counts by a separate dynamic program above); the asymptotic ones against `mpmath` at 60 digits at the exact statistic, relative `1e-11 + (stat + 1) 2^-53`; `Q_KS` against `mpmath`. **Controls** (must fall outside): no tie correction, no continuity correction, the classic `F` for Welch's, `chi2` on `k` degrees of freedom for `kruskal`, Fisher's tolerance of `1e-7` where the exact comparison matters (a table pair whose probabilities differ by less than that must exist in the generator) | outside the bound; a control inside it |
| R-C | **scipy**, reported with the bounds fixed here: `f_oneway`, `kruskal`, `mannwhitneyu(method="exact" and "asymptotic")`, `wilcoxon`, `ks_2samp`, `fisher_exact`; statistic to `1e-12` relative, p-values to `1e-9` (asymptotic) and `1e-12` (exact, where both are rationals rounded once); Welch's `F*` against `statsmodels`-free R formula in the oracle only (no R, no statsmodels dependency) | outside the bound on a well-conditioned family |
| R-D | **Calibration** under a true null, seeded, 4,000 replicates: `anova` (both) and `kruskal` at `k = 3, 5` groups of unequal sizes; `mannwhitney`, `wilcoxon`, `ks` at sizes from 5 to 1,000; the rejection rate inside the two-sided 99.9% binomial interval around the **exact size of the test as defined** (the sum of the probabilities of the rejection region for the exact tests, which is below the nominal level: stated; the normal and chi-square approximations are compared to the nominal level only where the corr gates found the approximation to hold, as in `docs/corr.md` section 8), and the p-values uniform (KS) where they are not on a lattice. Welch's `F*` under unequal variances and sizes must hold where the classic `F` must not | a wrong tail, correction or degrees of freedom: each control refused at some size |
| R-E | **Power**: a planted shift of known size is rejected at the rate the oracle gives (the exact distribution of `U` under a normal shift by simulation of the oracle with a fixed seed is not an independent reference: the reference is the closed-form power of the `t` test within the Pitman efficiency band where it applies, and otherwise printed, not gated) | printed |
| R-F | **Invariance**: 50 row permutations give the same bytes; swapping the groups negates `U - n_a n_b / 2`, the rank-biserial correlation and the odds ratio's log, leaves `p`; a monotone transformation of the values (`exp`, a shift, a scale) leaves every rank statistic and its p-value unchanged (`kruskal`, `mannwhitney`, `wilcoxon`, `ks`) | one byte differs |

## 6. Output, refusals

New rule tags **[choice]**: `stats.exact-unavailable` (`--exact yes` with ties or above the limit), `limit.exact-too-large` (`fisher` above `N = 10,000`); `stats.group-levels` is reused (`anova` and `kruskal` need `k >= 2` levels and at most `--max-groups`; `mannwhitney` and `ks` exactly two; `fisher` two levels in each column). A statistic that is undefined is `null` with a `reason` (`too-few-rows`, `variance-zero`, `odds-ratio-undefined`, `odds-ratio-infinite`), never a refusal. The new flags: `--exact auto|yes|no`, `--continuity auto|none`, `--zeros wilcox|pratt|zsplit`, and (reused) `--variance welch|pooled` for `anova`.

## 8. Decisions asked for, and what is proposed

The proposals stand unless overturned; each is a **[choice]**.

1. `anova` defaults to Welch's `F*` (`--variance pooled` for the classic one), as `t2` does. Alternative: the classic `F` by default, which is what `f_oneway` and most textbooks mean by "ANOVA".
2. The exact tests are used by default when there are no ties and the size allows (`--exact auto`), and the answer says so. Alternative: asymptotic by default, exact on request.
3. `fisher`'s two-sided p-value is the exact definition (probabilities not larger than the observed one) without the `1e-7` tolerance of R and scipy; the difference is documented and measured.
4. `ks` is two-sample only in v1; no Stephens correction.
5. `fisher` has no confidence interval in v1.
6. No post-hoc pairwise comparisons after `anova`/`kruskal` (Tukey, Dunn) in v1: a family of `mannwhitney` or `t2` tests with `--correct` is the available route; they are a separate design if wanted.
7. `corr --exact` (exact permutation p-values for Spearman and Kendall) is S4.7 and optional.
