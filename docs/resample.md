# `stats ci` and `stats resample`: intervals and seeded resampling (milestone S5)

**Status: designed 2026-10-27 before any code of S5; S5.1a (`ci prop`), S5.1b (`ci mean`, `ci diff`) and S5.1c (`ci corr`) are built and measured (sections 9 to 11); the rest is design only and nothing about it has been measured; what it reads from the code of S0 to S4 is marked [read], every bound and every default is a [choice] fixed before the first run and corrected in place, in this document, if a run shows it false, and what is not decided is [open].** Tags: **[read]** read from a document or source, **[measured]** printed by a script (none yet), **[choice]** a decision the maintainer can overturn, **[open]** not decided. This is the S5 row of `docs/design.md` section 10. The conventions of `docs/test.md` (the answer, the flags, the rule tags, the gates, the discipline of the mutants and the fingerprint) and of `docs/rank.md` are not repeated.

## 1. What it adds

Two verbs, both seeded where they draw. Intervals that have a closed form are answered with the same exact-where-it-decides arithmetic as the tests; the bootstrap is the one place where the answer is not an exact quantity but a **deterministic function of the data and the seed**, and the document says what that means and gates it.

| verb | kind | answers | exactness |
|---|---|---|---|
| `stats ci` | `mean` | the t interval for a mean | the same code as the interval of `test t1` **[read]** `lib/stest/answer.cho`; the bytes are equal |
| `stats ci` | `diff` | the Welch interval for a difference of means | as `test t2` |
| `stats ci` | `prop` | `--method wilson` (as `test prop`) or `clopper-pearson` (new) | the exact proportion; endpoints from the beta quantile of S0b **[read]** `special.dist` |
| `stats ci` | `corr` | the interval for Pearson's r by Fisher's z (new) | `r` is the exact one of `corr`; `atanh`, the interval on `z` and `tanh` in double-double or doubles, stated below |
| `stats ci` | `boot` | the percentile or BCa interval of a statistic of one column (or of a pair of columns, for `pearson`) | each replicate statistic is the correctly rounded value of the exact statistic of the resampled values; the interval is order statistics of the replicates |
| `stats resample` | `perm` | the permutation test of a difference of means or of medians between two groups, of Pearson's r between two columns, and the paired sign-flip test | the statistic of every permutation as above; the p-value is a count |
| `stats resample` | `boot` | the bootstrap distribution itself: the replicates' summary (mean, standard error, bias, quantiles) of a statistic | as `ci boot` |

The closed list of statistics (not an expression) **[choice]**: `mean`, `median`, `variance`, `sd`, `skewness`, `kurtosis`, `quantile` (with `--q`, a type of the nine of S1), `trimmed-mean` (with `--trim`), and, for two columns, `pearson`. Each is a statistic `describe` or `corr` already computes exactly **[read]**; the resampling does not change what it is.

## 2. Determinism: what a seeded answer promises **[choice]**

* **One stream per replicate.** Replicate `b` (0-based) draws its row indices from the stream `derive(derive(seed, kind), b)` of `ml.rng` **[read]** (SplitMix64, one int of state; `derive(h, c) = mix((h ^ c) + gold)`; the bounded draw is `uniform(z, m) = (output >> 1) mod m`, whose small bias for `m` not a power of two is stated in the answer's `assumptions.draw`: at most `m / 2^63` in probability for any one index, about `1.1e-13` at the default row limit of 1,000,000 and `1.1e-12` at the ceiling of 10,000,000 (arithmetic, not measured)). The `kind` constants are fixed by this document (`boot` 1, `perm` 2, `signflip` 3). Nothing depends on the order the replicates are computed in, so **adding threads can never change a replicate**, and the interval is a function of the sorted replicates only.
* **Replicates are exact where the statistic is.** The sum of a resample is the same whatever order the indices came in, and the exact accumulators (`acc.psum` / `acc.central`, **[read]** S0) take the multiset: so the mean, variance, sd, skewness, kurtosis of a replicate are the correctly rounded values of the exact statistic of that multiset, the same bits on every platform. `median`, `quantile`, `trimmed-mean` are order statistics of the multiset (exact selection, S1). `pearson` is the exact `r` of `corr` for the resampled pairs.
* **The interval is not exact and says so.** Its endpoints are order statistics of `R` replicates (percentile) or of a BCa adjustment of the level (below): a Monte Carlo quantity with a standard error that the answer reports (`mc_error`, from the binomial standard error of the position of the endpoint, **[choice]** as a diagnostic, not a guarantee). The bytes are reproducible; their meaning is the ordinary bootstrap's.
* **Cross-machine.** A fingerprint (`docs/xplat_fp.txt` lines `ci-boot`, `resample-perm`) on the three platforms of the CI workflow, as for every earlier slice.

## 3. `stats ci` **[choice]**

```
stats ci KIND FILE [--cols A,..] [--level 0.95] [KIND flags]
```

* **`mean`**, **`diff`** (`--group`), **`prop`** (`--success`, `--group` for two samples, `--method wilson|clopper-pearson|newcombe`): answered from the code of `test`, so that `ci mean` equals the `interval` of `test t1` byte for byte (gate). They are repeated here because "give me the interval" is a different question from "test this", and an agent should not have to run a test to read an interval. **Clopper-Pearson** is new: the lower endpoint is the `alpha/2` quantile of `Beta(x, n - x + 1)` and the upper the `1 - alpha/2` quantile of `Beta(x + 1, n - x)` (0 and 1 at `x = 0` and `x = n`), by the beta quantile of S0b, whose accuracy bound `kappa (1e-13 + 1e-15 |ln tau|)` relative to the quantile, with `kappa = 1 / min(1, a, b)` **[read]** `docs/numerics.md` section 9.2, is the bound of the endpoint.
* **`corr`** (`--cols A,B`, `--method pearson`): `z = atanh(r)`, standard error `1 / sqrt(n - 3)`, endpoints `tanh(z -+ q / sqrt(n - 3))` with `q = qnorm((1 + level) / 2)`; `n >= 4`, else `null` with `reason: "too-few-rows"`; `|r| = 1` has no interval (`reason: "perfect-correlation"`). The accuracy is that of `atanh` and `tanh` composed from `exp` and `ln` of `std.math` **[read]**: the bound is fixed before the gate (relative `1e-12`, **[choice]**). The Fisher interval is an approximation under bivariate normality and `assumptions` says so; Spearman and Kendall intervals are **not in v1** **[choice]**.
* **`boot`** (`--cols A` or `--cols A,B` with `--statistic pearson`, `--statistic S`, `--replicates R`, `--seed S`, `--method percentile|bca`): section 4.

The answer is `{"kind", "level", "estimate", "interval": {"low", "high", "method", ..}, "n", "method", "assumptions", "reason"}`; `estimate` is the statistic on the data itself, exact as above.

## 4. The bootstrap **[choice]**

* **Resample.** `n` rows drawn with replacement from the `n` rows with a value (for a pair of columns, the rows with both). The index of draw `j` of replicate `b` is `uniform(output number j of the stream of b, n)`.
* **Percentile.** Sort the `R` replicate statistics (as order keys: the doubles are ordered exactly). The endpoints are the order statistics at positions `floor(R alpha / 2)` and `ceil(R (1 - alpha / 2)) - 1` (0-based) **[choice]**: a stated type, not an interpolation, so that the endpoint is itself a replicate and no arithmetic touches it. (Hyndman and Fan's type 6/7 interpolations are not used for this reason; the choice is open to overturn.)
* **BCa.** Bias `z0 = qnorm(#{replicates < estimate} / R)` (ties counted as half), acceleration `a = sum (m - t_i)^3 / (6 (sum (m - t_i)^2)^(3/2))` from the `n` leave-one-out statistics `t_i` (`m` their mean), adjusted levels `alpha_k' = Phi(z0 + (z0 + z_k) / (1 - a (z0 + z_k)))`, the endpoints as above at `alpha_k'`. The leave-one-out statistics are exact (the correctly rounded statistic of the `n - 1` values); `z0`, `a` and the adjusted levels are computed in doubles from them, with the `qnorm` and `norm_cdf` of S0 **[read]**, so the BCa positions are a deterministic function of exact inputs and of correctly specified double arithmetic. `reason: "bca-undefined"` when `z0` is infinite (every replicate on one side) or the denominator vanishes; the interval then falls back to nothing (`null`), never silently to the percentile.
* **Bounds.** `--replicates` 100 to 100,000 (default 2,000 **[choice]**), `--max-work` the product `R n` at most 2^31 by default **[open: to be set from the measured cost of one replicate]**; over it `limit.too-much-work` (exit 8, repair: fewer replicates, or a column sample). `--seed` is required for the bootstrap (no default: an unseeded draw is not reproducible and the tool has no clock); the answer echoes it.
* **Not in v1** **[choice]**: the studentized (bootstrap-t) interval, the parametric bootstrap, block and cluster resampling, smoothed bootstraps, and bootstrap of an expression.

## 5. `stats resample` **[choice]**

* **`perm`**, two groups (`--cols A --group G`, `--statistic mean-diff|median-diff`): permute the group labels `R` times (a Fisher-Yates shuffle of the label vector from the replicate's stream), the statistic is the difference of group statistics; **two-sided** `p = (1 + #{|T*| >= |T|}) / (R + 1)` (Phipson and Smyth: a Monte Carlo p-value that is never 0 and is valid exactly), the comparison of two exact quantities for `mean-diff` (the difference of two exact rationals, compared as rationals, **no tolerance** as for `fisher`) and of two order statistics for `median-diff`. With `--replicates all` and at most 2^20 distinct label arrangements, the exact permutation p-value by enumeration (`p_method: "exact"`); else `"monte-carlo"` with the seed. 
* **`perm`, paired** (`--pairs A:B`): the sign-flip test of the differences, as above (`kind` 3).
* **`perm`, correlation** (`--cols A,B --statistic pearson`): permute one column.
* **`boot`**: the summary of the bootstrap distribution: `estimate`, `bias = mean(replicates) - estimate`, `se = sd(replicates)` (both from the exact sums of the replicates), the quantiles `--quantiles` as order statistics. 
* The answer says `p_method`, `replicates`, `seed`, `mc_error` (the binomial standard error of the p-value, `sqrt(p (1 - p) / R)`), as data.

## 6. Gates (written before the code; each can fail) **[choice]**

| id | gate | fails when |
|---|---|---|
| I-A | **Closed forms against `mpmath`** at 60 digits: Clopper-Pearson endpoints by root-finding on the exact beta cdf (relative `1e-12`), Fisher z endpoints (relative `1e-12`), `ci mean`/`diff`/`prop wilson` **byte equal** to the interval of `test` | outside the bound; one byte differs |
| I-B | **Coverage**, seeded, 4,000 replicates: Wilson, Clopper-Pearson (conservative: inside or above, printed against its exact coverage computed by summing binomial probabilities), Fisher z (bivariate normal), t for a mean and Welch for a difference; the rate inside the 99.9% binomial interval around the **exact coverage of the method** where it is computable and around the nominal level where it is the method's claim. **Controls** that must be refused: the Wald interval for a proportion, `z` for `t`, the Fisher interval with `n - 2` | a control inside the interval |
| I-C | **The bootstrap against an independent implementation**: a Python SplitMix64 and `derive` written from the specification (checked against the published vectors of `ml.rng` **[read]**), the same indices, `Fraction` statistics, and the replicates compared **bit for bit** for every statistic of the list at sizes from 2 to 200 with ties, constants, huge and subnormal values; then the percentile endpoints and the BCa positions (`z0`, `a`, adjusted levels from `mpmath` at 60 digits, the position by the same rounding rule) compared | one replicate bit differs; an endpoint is another replicate |
| I-D | **Thread and order invariance**: 1, 2, 3, 7 threads (if threaded), the rows shuffled, the columns named in another order: the same bytes; a different seed gives different replicates (a control: the gate must be able to tell) | one byte differs; two seeds equal |
| I-E | **Bootstrap coverage**: percentile and BCa intervals for the mean of a normal, an exponential and a lognormal sample at n = 20, 100, 500, 1,000 replicates of the experiment (each with R = 999, seeded), the rate printed with its 99.9% binomial interval; **printed, not gated against nominal** (the percentile bootstrap undercovers at small n for skewed data; that is a known property, and a gate that required 95% would be a false one). Gated: BCa's coverage at least the percentile's on the skewed families at n >= 100 (a stated expectation to be corrected in place if it is false) | measured, then corrected in place |
| I-F | **Permutation test calibration**: under a true null the rejection rate at 0.05 inside the binomial interval around `floor(0.05 (R + 1)) / (R + 1)` (the exact size of the Monte Carlo test); the exact (enumerated) p-value equals the oracle's rational bit for bit; a control with `p = #{...} / R` (without the +1) is refused | outside the interval; a control inside it |
| I-G | **Refusals and fuzz** (no input reaches a panic), **mutants** for every module (killed or each survivor explained), the check of their patterns, **the fingerprint** lines | an unexplained survivor; a differing line |

New rule tags **[choice]**: `limit.too-much-work`, `stats.seed-required`, `stats.statistic` (a statistic not on the list or not defined for the arguments). Flags: `--replicates`, `--seed`, `--statistic`, `--method`, `--trim`, `--q`, `--max-work`.

## 8. Decisions asked for, and what is proposed

1. `ci mean`, `ci diff` and `ci prop wilson` repeat the intervals of `test` with the same bytes (proposed), or `ci` offers only what `test` does not.
2. The statistic list of section 1 (proposed), or a shorter one (`mean`, `median`, `sd`, `pearson`) for v1.
3. The percentile endpoints are order statistics at `floor(R alpha / 2)` and `ceil(R (1 - alpha / 2)) - 1` (proposed), or an interpolated type.
4. BCa is in v1 (proposed), as its own slice, or deferred.
5. `--replicates` defaults to 2,000 for the bootstrap and 9,999 for the permutation test (proposed), with `--max-work` set from the measured cost of one replicate.
6. Threads: the first version is sequential and the invariance gate is trivially met; `conc` is added only if the measured cost of a realistic job (R = 10,000, n = 10,000) makes it necessary, and then under I-D (proposed).
7. No Spearman or Kendall interval and no studentized bootstrap in v1 (proposed).
8. The two-sided Monte Carlo p-value with the +1 (proposed), not the unadjusted `#/R`.

Open: the value of `--max-work` (to be measured); whether the draw's modulo bias should be removed by rejection sampling (it costs the one-to-one relation between a draw and an output, and the bias is at most `m / 2^63`, section 2; proposed: keep, stated); the `mc_error` field for an interval endpoint (a diagnostic, proposed).

## 9. S5.1a `ci prop`: what was built and measured **[measured]**

`stats ci prop --cols A,.. --success S [--group G] [--method wilson|clopper-pearson|newcombe] [--level L]` (`lib/csvx/ci.cho` the pass over the file, `lib/stest/answerci.cho` the entries). One sample: `--method wilson` (the default, the function of `test prop`) or `clopper-pearson` (new); two samples (`--group`, exactly two levels): Newcombe's hybrid score, the only method (`args.bad-value` for another, and for `newcombe` without `--group`). `--method` gained the values `wilson`, `clopper-pearson` and `newcombe` in the flag table; `corr` refuses them (`args.bad-value`). The answer is `{"ci": "prop", "success", ["group",] "level", "intervals": [{"column", "estimate", "interval": {"of", "method", "level", "estimate", "low", "high", "rounding"}, "n": {...}, ["levels",] ["reason"]}]}`; no proportion is a test and nothing here has a p-value.

* **Clopper-Pearson as built.** `low` = the `alpha/2` quantile of `Beta(x, n - x + 1)` (exactly 0 at `x = 0`), `high` = the `alpha/2` **upper-tail** quantile of `Beta(x + 1, n - x)` (exactly 1 at `x = n`), both by `special.quantile` (`beta_quantile`, `beta_quantile_upper`, **[read]** S0b); the upper tail is asked for directly, never as `1 - alpha/2`. `alpha/2` is the double `(10^k - num) / (2 10^k)` for the level `num / 10^k`. A quantile that does not converge is `reason: "interval-not-computed"`.
* **Wilson and Newcombe are `test prop`'s.** The `interval` object is written by the function `test prop` uses (`answer4.put_interval`; `wilson` and `critical` were made `pub`), so the two are the same bytes: **1,110 entries compared on 300 files (seed 2), 0 differ**, also the counts and the levels.
* **Clopper-Pearson against `mpmath`** (`scripts/gate_ci_prop.py`, the quantile by bisection on the regularised incomplete beta function at 50 digits, at the same double `alpha/2`): 277 intervals, each endpoint within the bound of `docs/numerics.md` 9.2, `kappa (1e-13 + 1e-15 |ln(alpha/2)|)` with `kappa = 1` (both shapes are at least 1); **the largest error is 0.040 of the bound**. Controls outside the bound: the Wald interval 128 of 128, Wilson's in place of Clopper-Pearson's 128 of 128. The rows shuffled and `--cols` in another order give the same entries.
* **Coverage** (`scripts/gate_ci_prop_coverage.py`, level 0.95, 600 values of `p`; the coverage of the program's intervals at every `x`, summed exactly with the binomial probabilities at 30 digits, so there is no sampling noise). Fixed before the run and holding: (1) Clopper-Pearson's coverage is at least the level at every `p` and `n` (minimum 0.9750, 0.9612, 0.9580, 0.9519, 0.9513 at `n` = 5, 10, 20, 40, 80); (2) Wilson's mean distance of the coverage from the level is smaller than Wald's at every `n` (0.0242 against 0.3139 at `n` = 5, 0.0069 against 0.0392 at `n` = 80); (3) the Wald control has a coverage minimum below the level minus 0.05 (0.0025 to 0.0392): the gate can tell. Wilson's minimum coverage on the grid is 0.835 to 0.853 at the five sizes (0.8352, 0.8405, 0.8528, 0.8485, 0.8395): stated, because "95% interval" does not mean 95% at every `p`.
* **Refusals.** 434 table cases (30 rule tags) and 1,000 fuzz files: no trap, no hang, every refusal tagged.
* **Mutants.** `scripts/mutants_ci.py`: 36, 35 killed and 1 explained in the script (`cp-code`, an unreachable guard: an argument, not a measurement). Four of the 36 were first found alive (the entry's `estimate`, the header's kind and level, and that guard); the gate gained the checks of the estimate and of the header, which killed three.
* **The 2,000-line limit.** `tools/stats/main.cho` is now 1,989 lines: the `ci` verb is the dispatch and the validation there and the rest in `lib/csvx/ci.cho`, as `lib/csvx/run.cho` took the group tests. **S5.1b cannot add its kinds to `main.cho`**: it starts with moving the glue of the earlier tests (`prop_one`, `chisq_one`, `fisher_one`, `t1_one`, `t2_one`) into `lib/csvx/`, with the mutant files that name them following, a refactor with no change of any byte of output (the fingerprint is the gate).

## 10. S5.1b `ci mean` and `ci diff`: what was built and measured **[measured]**

The rows shuffled and `--cols` in another order give the same entries.
* **Refusals.** 454 table cases (30 rule tags) and 1,000 fuzz files: no trap, no hang, every refusal tagged.
* **Mutants.** `scripts/mutants_ci.py` now holds 61 (36 of `ci prop`, 25 of `mean` and `diff`): 59 killed and 2 explained in the script (`cp-code`, an unreachable guard; `mean-variance-zero`, equivalent: with every value equal the half-width is `q * 0` on either branch). ## 11. S5.1c `ci corr`: what was built and measured **[measured]**

`stats ci corr [--cols A,B,..] [--missing pairwise|listwise] [--level L] [--max-pairs N]`: Pearson's `r` of each pair (the exact `r` of `corr`, one rounding of an exact rational, `corr.pearson.pair`) and the Fisher z interval `tanh(atanh r -+ q / sqrt(n - 3))`, `q` the normal quantile of `(1 + level) / 2` (`answer4.critical`, the one `test prop` uses). `--method` other than `pearson` is `args.bad-value` (no Spearman or Kendall interval in v1, section 3). **As built:** the glue of `corr` (`corr_one`) takes the flag `ci` as the tests' glue did; the header is `{"ci": "corr", "rows", "level", "method", "missing", "columns", "intervals": [...]}` (no `adjustment`), the entries `{"x", "y", "n", "estimate", "interval": {"of": "correlation", "method": "fisher-z", "level", "estimate", "low", "high", "rounding"}, ["reason"]}` written by `answerci.put_corr`. `atanh` is `0.5 log1p(2 r / (1 - r))` (odd extension for `r < 0`) and `tanh` is `expm1(2 |x|) / (expm1(2 |x|) + 2)` (1 beyond `|x| = 20`), so the digits of a small `r` or a small argument are kept. Null intervals carry a `reason`: `constant-column`, `perfect-correlation` (`|r| = 1` decided by the integers `N^2 = Lx Ly`, also for `n = 2`), `too-few-rows` (`n < 4`).

* **The interval is a function of the double `r`.** Near `|r| = 1` the conditioning is `1 / (1 - r^2)`: the rounding of `r` (relative 1.1e-16) moves `atanh r` by `1.1e-16 r / (1 - r^2)`, which is not small for `r = 1 - 1e-11`. The gate therefore judges the endpoints from **the double `r` the program prints**, at 60 digits, not from the exact rational; the document says it, and the estimate itself is bit for bit the exact one.
* **The bound, fixed before the first run:** relative `1e-12` of `max(|endpoint|, (1 - endpoint^2) max(|z|, q se))`, the second term being the rounding of the argument `z -+ q se` seen through `tanh` (near 0 or near 1 a relative bound on the endpoint alone could not be met by any double formula), and the normal quantile's own `2e-13` entering through `q se`.
* **Gate** (`scripts/gate_ci_corr.py`, seed 2, 300 files of `gate_corr.py` in both `--missing` modes and levels from 0.5 to 0.999, plus pairs with `r` within 1e-9 of 1 and -1, of the size of 1e-9, and `r` near 0): 2,102 pairs, 1,171 intervals, **0 differ**; `n`, the estimate (bit for bit), the reasons exactly; the largest endpoint error is **0.00081 of the bound** (about 1e-15 relative). Controls outside the bound: `n - 2` in place of `n - 3` 983 of 1,003, the endpoints of `z` without `tanh` 1,163 of 1,163. The rows shuffled give the same bytes; `--method spearman` and `kendall` are refused. scipy's `pearsonr(...).confidence_interval` agrees on the one example looked at by hand (`low` differs in the 15th digit); it is not a gate.
* **Refusals.** 469 table cases (30 rule tags) and 1,000 fuzz files: no trap, no hang, every refusal tagged.
* **Mutants.** `scripts/mutants_ci.py` now holds 79 (18 of `ci corr`, 61 before): 77 killed and 2 explained in the script; the 18 of `ci corr` and the two patterns they changed were all killed at the first run (20 of 20).

## 12. S5.2a `ci boot`: the design of the first bootstrap slice (written before the code) **[choice]**

Section 4 fixed the method; this section fixes what the first slice builds, the numbers a gate can check, and what is left for the next one. **S5.2a:** `stats ci boot` with `--method percentile` and the statistics `mean`, `median`, `variance` and `sd`. **S5.2b** (not in this slice): `skewness`, `kurtosis`, `quantile`, `trimmed-mean` and, for two columns, `pearson`. **S5.3:** BCa. The cut is the cost of the exact statistic of a multiset: the first four need the sums of `x` and `x^2` of the resample and an exact selection, which `describe` already has; the others need either the third and fourth power sums or a pair of columns, each its own gate.

```
stats ci boot FILE --cols A,B,.. --seed S [--statistic mean|median|variance|sd] [--replicates R] [--level L] [--max-work W]
```

* **Streams, fixed here so that an independent implementation can reproduce them.** SplitMix64 as in `ml.rng` **[read]**: `gold = 0x9E3779B97F4A7C15`, `mix` the finaliser (`z ^= z >> 30; z *= 0xBF58476D1CE4E5B9; z ^= z >> 27; z *= 0x94D049BB133111EB; z ^= z >> 31`, logical shifts, products modulo 2^64), `derive(h, c) = mix((h xor c) + gold)`. Replicate `b` (from 0) has the stream seed `s_b = derive(derive(seed, 1), b)` (`1` is the kind `boot`); its draw `j` (from 0) is `z = mix(s_b + (j + 1) gold)`, the index `i = (z >> 1) mod n` (the shift logical): the row `i` among the `n` present values of the column, in file order. `n` draws make a resample. `--seed` is a non-negative integer below 2^62, **required** (`stats.seed-required`): an unseeded draw is not reproducible and the tool has no clock.
* **The statistic of a resample is exact.** The multiset of the `n` values is summed by the exact accumulators of S0/S1 (`acc.psum`): `mean = S1 / n`, `variance = (n S2 - S1^2) / (n (n - 1))` (the `ddof` 1 of `describe`), each correctly rounded once (`central.ratio_bits`); `sd = sqrt(variance)` of the double, as `describe` does; `median` is the middle value for odd `n` and for even `n` the exact half-sum of the two middle values rounded once, found by sorting the order keys of the resample (exact). So a replicate is a function of the multiset only, and the same on every platform. `estimate` is the statistic of the data itself, by the same functions.
* **Percentile endpoints, in exact integers.** With the level `num / 10^k`: the `R` replicates are sorted (as order keys), `low` is the replicate at 0-based position `floor(R (10^k - num) / (2 10^k))` and `high` the one at `ceil(R (10^k + num) / (2 10^k)) - 1`; both positions are printed, so the endpoint is itself a replicate and no arithmetic touches it. At least 100 and at most 100,000 replicates (default 2,000, `args.bad-value` otherwise); `R n` at most `--max-work` (default 2^31, **[open]** until the cost of one replicate is measured, section 8) else `limit.too-much-work` (exit 8). A column of fewer than 2 values is `null` with `reason: "too-few-rows"`; a replicate whose variance is undefined cannot occur for `n >= 2`.
* **The answer** is `{"ci": "boot", "statistic", "method": "percentile", "level", "replicates", "seed", "rows", "assumptions": {"draw", "stream"}, "intervals": [{"column", "estimate", "interval": {"of", "method": "bootstrap-percentile", "level", "estimate", "low", "high", "positions": {"low", "high"}, "rounding"}, "n": {...}, ["reason"]}]}`; no `mc_error` in this slice (a diagnostic proposed in section 2; it can be added without changing a byte of the rest, and is open).
* **Gate (I-C, I-D for these statistics, written before the code).** An independent implementation in Python: SplitMix64 from the specification above, checked against the published first output for seed 0 (`0xE220A8397B1DCDAF`) and against the vectors of `ml.rng`; the same indices; the statistic of each resample as a `Fraction`, rounded once; **every replicate bit-equal** needs the replicates themselves in the answer, which the gate gets from a debug-free route: the endpoints and their positions are replicates, and the gate recomputes all `R` and checks that the printed `low` and `high` are the values at the printed positions of the sorted list, and that the positions follow the rule. Invariance: the rows shuffled **change** the stream's meaning (draw `j` picks the `i`th row in file order) and therefore the replicates; what must not change is the answer for the same file bytes, at any `--threads` (none in this slice) and across `--cols` orders. Controls (must fall outside): another seed; `kind` 2 in place of 1; `derive` with the draw index in place of the replicate index; the percentile positions with `floor(R alpha)` for both; the `n - 1` of a mean in place of `n`. Refusals, fuzz, mutants, the fingerprint lines `ci-boot`.

### 12.1 S5.2a results (measured)

* **Gate** (`scripts/gate_ci_boot.py`, seed 5, 200 files of `gate_test2.py`'s shapes, the four statistics in turn, seeds up to 2^62 - 1, 100 to 800 replicates): 326 entries (13 null), 0 problems. Every printed `low` and `high` is bit-equal to the replicate at the printed position of the oracle's sorted list; the positions, the estimates and the reasons are the expected ones; the same bytes on a second run and with `--cols` in another order. The oracle's SplitMix64 reproduces the published outputs `0xe220a8397b1dcdaf`, `0x6e789e6aa1b965f4`, `0x06c45d188009454f` for seed 0. Controls left the answer different on: another seed 170 of 313, kind 2 178 of 313, indices exchanged 175 of 313, floor for the high position 80 of 313, `n - 1` for the mean 80 of 80 (each at least a tenth). Dev box, 4 cores, load low.
* **Cost** (one run each, 1,000 values, 1,000 replicates = 10^6 draws, same box): mean 0.064 s, median 0.047 s, variance 0.111 s, so about 50 to 110 ns a draw. The default `--max-work` 2^31 is then roughly 2 to 4 minutes at the largest. It stays a choice, not a limit derived from a requirement **[open]**.
* **Refusals.** 497 table cases and fuzz files of the gate (new tags `stats.seed-required`, `limit.too-much-work`); no trap, no hang. A seed with an empty value is refused by `toolbox.cli` itself.
* **Mutants.** `scripts/mutants_boot.py`: 56; 55 killed, 1 explained in the script (`seed-empty`: the empty-value guard in `parse_seed` is shadowed by the flag reader's own refusal). Four survived the first run and were killed by new cases: `--replicates 100000`, a column of one value (estimate for the mean and median, null for variance and sd), and `n < 2` of the variance.
* **Fingerprint** `ci-boot`: 6 lines in `docs/xplat_fp.txt`, to be confirmed on the three platforms by CI.

## 13. S5.2b `ci boot`: skewness, kurtosis, quantile and trimmed mean (written before the code) **[choice]**

Four statistics are added to section 12 with the same streams, positions and answer; only the statistic of a resample is new. **Pearson's r of two columns moves to S5.2c**: its resample is a resample of ROWS (the same indices for both columns) and its statistic needs the exact cross sums, which is a gate of its own.

```
stats ci boot FILE --cols A,B,.. --seed S --statistic skewness|kurtosis|quantile|trimmed-mean [--prob P] [--quantile-type T] [--trim X] ...
```

* **skewness, kurtosis.** The functions of `describe` (`central.skew_kurt` on the four exact power sums of the resample: the population skewness `m3 / m2^(3/2)` and the excess kurtosis `m4 / m2^2 - 3`, each rounded once). **A resample of a column that is not constant can be constant** (every draw the same row: probability `n^(1-n)`, 11% for n = 3, 1.6% for n = 4), and then the statistic does not exist. The rule is a **choice**: no replicate is dropped or invented. If any of the `R` replicates is undefined, the interval is `null` with `reason: "replicate-undefined"` and the count of undefined replicates in `undefined`; if the data itself is constant, the estimate is `null` and the reason is `variance-zero`. A replicate beyond the double is `statistic-beyond-double`, as in section 12. So small columns get a refusal that says why, never a percentile of a smaller set than the one printed.
* **quantile.** `--prob P` (a decimal from 0 to 1 with at most 9 decimals, required with this statistic and refused with the others, `args.bad-value`) and `--quantile-type T` (1 to 9, default 7), the function of `describe` on the sorted keys of the resample. `median` of section 12 is `quantile` with type 7 and `P = 0.5`.
* **trimmed-mean.** `--trim X` (a decimal in `[0, 0.5)` with at most 9 decimals, default 0.1, refused with the other statistics when given): in the sorted resample `g = floor(n num / 10^k)` values are dropped from each end (an exact integer) and the mean of the other `n - 2g` is the exact sum over `n - 2g`, rounded once. `X = 0` is the mean; `n - 2g >= 1` always (`2g <= 2 n X < n`).
* **The answer** has the keys of section 12; for `quantile` the header also carries `p` and `type`, for `trimmed-mean` `trim`, as the decimals given.
* **Gate** (I-C, I-D): the oracle's `boot_stat` extended with the four (the shape moments of `gate_describe.shape_moments`, the Fraction quantile of `gate_quantile.hf`, the trimmed mean as a Fraction), every replicate recomputed, the printed `low` and `high` bit-equal at the printed positions; the undefined-replicate rule checked by counting the constant resamples in the oracle. Controls (each must leave the answer different on at least a tenth of the cases): the type 7 quantile in place of the given type; the trim `floor(n X)` replaced by `round`; the sample kurtosis (not excess); skewness of the sample variance (`n - 1`).
